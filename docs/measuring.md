# Measuring your rim and hub

Every wrong answer a spoke calculator gives comes from one of these four
numbers. The maths is not the hard part — the measuring is.

A millimetre of error in the flange diameter moves the answer by about half a
millimetre. Two millimetres of error moves it a whole stock size.

---

## ERD — effective rim diameter

**What it is:** the diameter of the circle the *spoke ends* reach, not the
diameter of the rim.

This is the number people get wrong most often. A "700c" rim is 622 mm at the
bead seat, but its ERD is typically 590–610 mm, and on a deep aero rim it can be
under 560 mm. The nominal size tells you nothing useful here.

**How to measure it:**

1. Take two spokes you already know the length of — ideally with nipples
   threaded fully on.
2. Thread them into opposite holes in the rim, nipples seated as they would be
   in a built wheel.
3. Measure from the end of one spoke to the end of the other, across the middle
   of the rim.
4. `ERD = that measurement + (2 × spoke length)`.

If the manufacturer publishes an ERD, measure anyway and treat theirs as a
cross-check. Published figures are often for a different model year, and some
are measured to the top of the nipple rather than the spoke end.

> **The nipple question.** ERD is conventionally measured to where the *spoke
> end* sits when the nipple is fully threaded. If you measure to the top of the
> nipple head instead, you will be about 4 mm out, which is two stock sizes.

---

## Flange diameter

**What it is:** the diameter of the circle through the *centres of the spoke
holes* in the hub flange — not the outside of the flange disc.

**How to measure it:** measure from the centre of one hole to the centre of the
hole opposite, straight across the axle. On a flange with an odd hole layout,
measure from the far edge of one hole to the near edge of the hole opposite —
that gives you centre-to-centre without having to guess where the centre is.

A caliper across the outside of the flange will read several millimetres too
large and will give you spokes that are too short.

---

## Centre to flange

**What it is:** the distance from the **centreline of the hub** — the middle of
the wheel, where the rim sits — out to the flange.

**How to measure it:**

1. Measure the overall locknut-to-locknut width (the "OLD" — 100 mm for most
   front hubs, 130/135 mm for older rears, 142 or 148 mm Boost for modern ones).
2. Measure from the outside of one locknut to the near face of each flange.
3. `centre to flange = (OLD / 2) − (locknut to flange)`.

Measure to the middle of the flange's thickness if you want to be precise, but
the error from measuring to a face is small.

> **This is measured from the middle of the hub, not from the end.** If you
> enter a locknut-to-flange distance directly you will get a badly dished,
> badly wrong answer. The calculator flags anything over 60 mm for this reason.

---

## Spoke hole diameter

The diameter of the holes in the hub flange, usually **2.6 mm** for standard
2.0 mm spokes. It is only used to correct for the fact that a spoke is measured
from the inside of its elbow, which sits half a hole-diameter closer to the
axle than the hole's centre.

Leave it at 2.6 unless you have measured otherwise. Getting it wrong by half a
millimetre moves the answer by a quarter of a millimetre — which is the one
input here that it is safe to be casual about.

---

## A sanity check before you order

- Both sides of a front rim-brake wheel should come out within a fraction of a
  millimetre of each other.
- A rear wheel's drive side is always the shorter of the two.
- Lengths for a 700c wheel land somewhere around 290 mm. If you are seeing 260
  or 320, re-measure the ERD before you buy anything.

When two sides land within 1 mm of each other, the calculator says so — that is
your cue to order one length for the whole wheel.
